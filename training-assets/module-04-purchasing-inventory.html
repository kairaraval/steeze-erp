Purchasing & Inventory

How materials get from a closed-won deal to the production floor — requests, approvals, purchase orders, payment, receiving, and keeping stock accurate — plus the sublimation blocks process end to end.

RolePurchasing Admin Reports toAGM / CEO Works daily withSales · Production · Accounting Read time~25 min

01 — The role

What purchasing does at Steeze

Purchasing makes sure production never stops for want of materials — and that nothing is over-bought or paid for twice. You are the bridge between what Sales sells, what Production needs, and what Accounting pays for.

Your job has three jobs inside it:

  • Decide what to buy — turn each project's material list into real purchase quantities, after checking what is already in stock.
  • Buy it well — choose the right supplier, consolidate orders, get the right price and payment terms, and raise the purchase order.
  • Land it correctly — receive the goods, update stock, and get materials to the right project — so inventory always matches reality.
The golden rule

Buy only what's needed after checking stock, from the right supplier, with the money side handed cleanly to Accounting — and keep inventory honest. Everything below is how we do that step by step.

Step 1 · Requests come in

  1. A request is created

    Most Purchase Requests (PRs) appear automatically: when Sales marks a deal Closed Won, a production PR is created from the techpack's material list (BOM); when a sample is approved, a sample PR appears. Anyone in Sales can also raise a manual request ("Request for Purchasing").

    Owner: Sales / the system

  2. It lands in Purchase Requests

    Each PR shows the client, sales owner, techpack number, fabric, quantity, due date, and the material lines needed. Open View techpack to see the full spec before buying anything.

    Owner: Purchasing

Step 2 · Review & approve (Minda)

  1. Check stock coverage, line by line

    Each material line shows a stock strip: On-hand · Reserved · Available, and whether it's Fully covered by stock, Partial, or No stock — must buy. For sublimation fabrics it also shows 🧱 Blocks available (see the blocks section). If stock already covers it, Pull from stock instead of buying.

  2. Set what to buy

    For lines that need buying, set the buy quantity, unit price, and supplier. Accurate unit prices matter — they feed project costing and Accounting's valuation.

  3. Approve

    When the request is right, Minda approves it. That hands it to you (Purchasing Admin) for PO processing, and you get a notification.

    Owner: Purchasing (Minda)

Step 3 · Materials Queue → Purchase Order (you)

  1. Open the Materials Queue

    This is your workbench. Approved request lines from all projects are grouped by supplier, so you can buy one supplier's items for several projects in a single order.

  2. Create a draft PO per supplier

    Click Create draft PO on a supplier's card. The system builds a purchase order with that supplier's lines pre-filled. Review quantities and prices.

  3. Set payment terms and expected pay-date

    On the PO, set the payment terms (e.g. 50% down payment, Net 15, Net 30) and the expected pay-date. This is how Accounting knows when the money is due.

  4. Finalize and send to the supplier

    Confirm prices and availability with the supplier, finalize the PO, and send it.

    Owner: Purchasing Admin (you)

Step 4 · Request for Payment → Accounting

  1. Raise the Request for Payment

    On the finalized PO, click Request for Payment. Attach the proof of transaction / supplier invoice. This creates an RFP that goes straight to Accounting.

  2. Accounting processes it

    Accounting reviews the RFP (For Approval → For Payment) and schedules it per the payment terms. The CEO authorizes the actual release of funds in the bank.

Important

Raising a Request for Payment is not paying. Purchasing never moves money. You hand the figures and proof to Accounting; the CEO releases the funds.

Step 5 · Receive the goods

  1. Open the PO and click Receive

    When the delivery arrives, open the PO and record what came in — the quantity received per line (partial deliveries are fine; receive again when the rest arrives).

  2. Stock in and allocate

    Receiving adds the quantities to inventory and lets you allocate / issue them to the projects the lines were for. On-hand updates automatically.

    Owner: Purchasing Admin (you)

Step 6 · Stock out to production

  1. Issue materials to the project

    In Stock Out, issue items from inventory to a specific job/project. On-hand drops and a stock-out is logged. Production can now work.

  2. Confirm in Stock Movements

    At the end of the day, open Stock Movements and check that all ins (receipts) and outs (issuances) landed correctly.

05 — The money side

How Purchasing and Accounting fit together

Purchasing and Accounting share one job split in two: Purchasing decides what to buy and from whom; Accounting handles the money. The handoff is always the same document trail — the PO and the Request for Payment.

Purchasing ownsAccounting owns
What to buy, how much, which supplier, the priceWhether/when it's paid, against which budget
Payment terms & expected pay-date on the POScheduling payment to match those terms
Raising the Request for Payment + attaching proofApproving the RFP, checking the supplier invoice
Accurate unit costs on every itemInventory valuation & project costing from those costs

Two money paths

  • Project materials → PO → Request for Payment → Accounting → CEO releases funds. This is your everyday path.
  • Non-project / general spend (office, tools, anything not on a project) → raise a Budget Request first. The CEO approves it, and you'll be notified of the decision. Only then buy.
Why costs must be right

Every unit price you enter flows into Accounting's numbers and into each project's cost. A sloppy price isn't just a purchasing detail — it makes the company's margins wrong. Keep prices current.

06 — Stock accuracy

Keeping inventory correct

Inventory is only useful if it matches the warehouse. Two habits keep it honest: receive everything through the PO, and issue everything through Stock Out. If stock moves without a movement logged, the numbers drift and purchasing starts guessing.

The buckets

Inventory is organized into buckets so like items stay together:

Reorder points

Set a reorder level on items you always keep in stock (common trims, house fabrics). When on-hand falls below it, that item needs buying before a project forces a rush order. Review low-stock items weekly.

07 — Sublimation

The sublimation blocks process

Sublimation fabric is special: we buy it by the roll (kg) but use it by the cut block (pieces). That's why fabric is tracked at two levels, connected by a cutting step. This keeps inventory correct and makes future estimates accurate.

Buy rollown PO · kg
→
Receiveroll stock, kg
→
Cut to blockspieces + remnant
→
Block stockgeneral, pcs
→
Stock outto project

  1. Buy and receive the roll

    Sublimation fabric is stocked on rolls, usually bought on its own purchase order. Receive it into Fabrics stock in kilos, as normal.

  2. Cut rolls to blocks

    In Stock Out → ✂ Cut to blocks: pick the roll, enter the kilos consumed, choose a block size (add new sizes with + New size — e.g. "Body block 60×90"), enter the blocks produced, and the remnant / side-cut kilos.

    On save, the system automatically: drops the roll's kilos, creates the block item (named by fabric + color + size) and adds the pieces to Fabric Blocks, and adds the side cuts to Remnants.

  3. Stock out blocks to the project

    Production pulls from the general block stock — issue blocks to the job in Stock Out, just like any item.

On the Purchase Request

For a sublimation project, a fabric line shows both the roll stock and a 🧱 Blocks available line with the count by size. So before buying more roll, you can see whether cut blocks already cover the job. (The blocks line only appears once blocks have actually been cut.)

Why this matters

The PR's kilos stay a planning estimate. Real consumption is tracked in blocks. Over time this builds a true picture of how many blocks a kilo yields — so estimates stop being guesses and buying gets tighter.

Don't forget the remnants

Always record the side cuts when you cut blocks. Remnants are usable fabric — logging them means we reuse leftovers instead of re-buying. Ignore only true scrap.

08 — Rhythm

Your daily & weekly routine

Every day

  • Open the Purchasing Home — clear the action tiles (requests waiting, materials needed, POs to receive).
  • Work the Materials Queue — turn approved requests into POs per supplier.
  • Raise Request for Payment for POs that are due, with proof attached.
  • Receive deliveries the same day they arrive, and allocate to projects.
  • Check Stock Movements — confirm the day's ins and outs are correct.

Every week

  • Review low-stock / reorder items and replenish house materials before a rush.
  • Reconcile open POs — chase deliveries that are late.
  • Check sublimation block stock vs. remnants, and cut ahead if demand is coming.
  • Confirm supplier prices in Resources are still current.

09 — Pitfalls

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Buying before checking stock. Always read the stock strip first and Pull from stock when it's covered.
  • One PO per project per supplier. Don't — the Materials Queue exists to consolidate a supplier's items across projects into one order.
  • Receiving outside the PO. If goods arrive and you don't receive them on the PO, inventory is wrong and the PO looks unpaid/undelivered.
  • Issuing stock without Stock Out. Hand-carrying materials to the floor without logging it breaks on-hand counts.
  • Forgetting payment terms. No terms/pay-date on the PO means Accounting can't schedule payment and suppliers chase you.
  • Treating the PR kilos as truth for sublimation. It's an estimate. Inventory truth is the roll kg and the block count.

10 — Terms

Glossary

  • PR — Purchase RequestThe material need for a project; what gets reviewed and approved before buying.
  • BOMBill of Materials — the list of materials from a techpack that a production PR is built from.
  • PO — Purchase OrderThe order sent to a supplier, with items, prices, and payment terms.
  • Materials QueueApproved PR lines grouped by supplier, ready to turn into POs.
  • RFP — Request for PaymentWhat Purchasing raises from a PO to hand the payment to Accounting.
  • Payment termsWhen the supplier is paid (e.g. 50% DP, Net 15, Net 30).
  • Stock-in / Stock-outAdding stock (from receiving) / issuing stock (to production). Both are logged as movements.
  • Reorder levelThe on-hand quantity below which an item needs re-buying.
  • BlockA standard-size piece cut from a sublimation roll; counted in pieces, held in general stock.
  • RemnantUsable side-cut fabric left after cutting blocks; tracked in kilos.
  • Budget RequestApproval route (via the CEO) for non-project / general spend.

11 — Check yourself

Knowledge check

Answer all six. 80% to pass.

Q1Before buying a material line, what should you do first?

Stock may already cover it — always read On-hand / Available first and Pull from stock when you can.

Q2Where do you turn approved requests into purchase orders?

The Materials Queue groups approved lines by supplier so you consolidate into one PO.

Q3Raising a "Request for Payment" means…

Purchasing never moves money. Accounting processes the RFP; the CEO releases funds.

Q4Sublimation fabric is bought in ___ and used in ___.

Rolls are bought by weight; blocks are cut from them and used by the piece.

Q5When you cut a roll to blocks, the side cuts should be…

Enter the remnant kilos on the cut — they go to Remnants stock and get reused.

Q6A non-project office purchase should go through…

Non-project spend goes through a Budget Request for CEO approval first.
